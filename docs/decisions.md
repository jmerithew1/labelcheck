## 2026-08-12 — robustness at scale: four verdict gates, a real-label corpus, and three measurements that measured the wrong thing

Scaled testing from 120 synthetic images to **1,360 synthetic** (34 scoreable labels × 40 degradation conditions) plus **200 real approved TTB COLA labels** harvested from the public registry (`samples/tools/harvest-ttb.mjs`; images gitignored, scored summaries in `docs/robustness-matrix.json` and `docs/real-labels.json`). **Cross-template coverage first**: every violation previously lived on the bourbon template, so the missed-violation rate had been measured on one font, one layout, one warning placement; the five defect types now exist on wine, can and gin too (21 violations across 4 templates, 6 → 21). **Four verdict gates added, all obeying the same rule — a red FAIL must be deterministic, corroborated and showable, and everything else is amber REVIEW**: (1) *deviation-agreement* — two readings must agree on *which* word differs, since damage produces a different misread each time while a real alteration reproduces; (2) *legibility* — no word-for-word claim on text that could not actually be read, after a real one-word swap passed clean on 10 of 40 degraded variants because the reader reconstructs the memorised sentence; (3) *disputed absence* — `fail_missing` downgrades when the typography pass reports the same pixels as illegible, since one reading seeing nothing and another seeing something unreadable is disagreement, not evidence; (4) *statement-boundary trimming* — the wording comparison is scoped to the statement's own boundaries after 6 of 196 real labels came back with a sulfite declaration appended or a neighbouring block captured. **The real corpus earned its place immediately**, finding two defects no synthetic image could: real labels print "(1)According" and "defects.(2)" with no space (12 of 14 sampled wording failures were that and nothing else — spacing is typography, not wording, so it is now a note), and transcription runs past the statement on crowded artwork. Ground truth came free and is the point: an approved COLA is compliant by definition, so any warning failure on one is a false rejection, measurable without hand-transcribing anything. **Three times a number looked alarming because the harness measured the wrong thing** — an all-errors run reported "0 false rejections · 0 missed violations" (a perfect-looking score produced by 120 consecutive HTTP 500s); applicant filenames were trusted as ground truth, producing a fake 23% false-rejection rate until inspection showed files named "back" containing front-label artwork; and the bold matrix read the raw server advisory (29% wrong) instead of the shipped stroke-width gate, which re-scored offline at **3 silent misses in 160 (1.9%)**. Each was caught by checking before reporting, so `samples/tools/robustness-matrix.mjs` now refuses to print a score when >2% of checks error, asserts scored-count equals queued-count, and names the layer it measured; `npm run verify`, a CI workflow, and `scripts/check-doc-counts.mjs` (which fails the build when prose asserts a count the repo contradicts — test counts have now drifted three times) close the rest. **Honesty corrections**: the day-one spike's finding that reconstruction risk "did not materialise" was true on sharp renders and false on degraded ones; "zero false rejections" held only at n=15 and is now 7.5% of 560 compliant samples at n=1,360; and adjacent-text-before-the-warning was downgraded from `fail_wording` to a review note, because a transcription cannot distinguish a label printing text beside the statement from the reader over-capturing it, and an uncorroborated cause must not reject a compliant application. **Rejected alternatives**: making bold a hard fail — rejected by owner, it is the one requirement with no deterministic ground truth and a false rejection there is unrecoverable; reviving the shelved deterministic bold research — rejected because it is already in production (`lib/compare/boldGate.ts`) and the belief that it was not shipped was simply wrong; adding beverage-type intake fields — rejected, the six existing fields covered every product class in the real corpus and class-conditional rules are past the brief; trusting attachment filenames as ground truth — rejected, applicants mislabel their own uploads; re-running the full 1,360-image matrix per iteration — rejected in favour of targeted subsets and zero-API-cost offline re-scoring.

## 2026-08-12 — ship-readiness sweep: 3 blockers fixed, repo cleaned, bold pass made opt-in on large batches

Ran a 4-lane sweep over the whole feature set against production (API + error handling, batch UI, single-check UI, repo/docs). **Blockers found and fixed**: (1) *drag-to-pan was broken with a real mouse* — the label `<img>` started a native HTML5 image drag on the first pointermove, firing `pointercancel` and freezing the gesture after a few pixels; every gesture bought exactly one move's worth of pan, and with scrollbars hidden the sides of a zoomed label were unreachable. Fixed with `draggable={false}` + `preventDefault` on pointerdown. Note the synthetic-PointerEvent test used during the original build PASSES against the broken build — scripted pointermoves never trigger `dragstart` — so the lesson is that pointer-gesture verification needs real CDP mouse input, not dispatched events. (2)+(3) two stale current-state doc counts (81 tests → 85, 18 labels → 20). **Minors fixed**: the bold chip counted rows the strip excluded (they now share one `boldPendingRow` predicate, so dot, chip and strip always describe the same set); the "Checked in" figure tripled after the async confirmation merged (the confirmation is now reported separately as "· second reading N.Ns", keeping the headline the time to the verdict on screen); the >60-row opt-in bold pass, the agent ruling and its `agent_review` CSV column, the undo affordance and the Need-review bucketing were all shipped but undocumented — now in approach.md and rubric U3/C9. **Honesty additions** to the trade-offs: the ~5s figure is a median (tail reached 9.6s across 14 live runs, one run needed a retry), and the relative type-size advisory is non-deterministic (~1 run in 3 on clean-match). **Repo cleanup**: 375 generated spike artifacts (multisignal-out, r2/r3-out, croplens-out, locate-preview) and the 5MB tesseract language blob were untracked and gitignored — they are regenerable from `samples/tools/*.mjs` and the evidence summaries the docs cite live in `docs/*.json`. **Rejected alternatives**: keeping the eager bold pass on every batch size (hundreds of unrequested locate calls on a 250-label surge dump, undoing the batch path's deliberate 2-calls-per-label design); leaving the spike PNGs tracked "as evidence" (the JSON summaries are the evidence; the images are re-renderable and inflate a clean clone).

## 2026-08-11 — crop-lens AI signal tested and REJECTED; strip becomes attention-only

Two owner directives closed the bold-gate work. (1) **Crop lens**: hypothesis was that feeding the AI the same 3x-upscaled warning crop the pixel measurement uses would sharpen its signal and raise gate coverage. Measured (`samples/tools/bold-croplens.mjs`, evidence `docs/bold-croplens-results.json`): the crop-fed AI scored WORSE than the full-label AI (60 vs 71 right of 96 scored; 10 unclears vs 0), and the gate rebuilt on it produced **one confident mistake on held-out validation** (val-cola-calibri-reg gated bold, truth not-bold) — a hard violation of the zero-confident-mistake rule. Rejected; the round-2 configuration (full-label AI signal) remains the shipped gate. Interpretation: the model uses whole-label context, and interpolated upscaling distorts stroke appearance for a vision model even though it helps pixel measurement. (2) **Attention-only strip**: the Confirm bold strip now appears BY ITSELF when labels need a human glance and disappears when nothing does; it lists only the pending rows ("6 of 11 verified by measurement — these are the 5 that need your eyes"), warning bands auto-fetch after the run so the machine gate resolves rows before anything is asked of the human, and unmeasurable rows (PDFs, no located band) route to the strip explicitly. Row markers distinguish provenance: "bold ✓ measured" (machine) vs "bold ✓" (human). Because decided cards leave the strip immediately, every decision raises a transient toast with **Undo** (restores the previous state and returns the card) — a misclick on a compliance decision must never be permanent. **Rejected alternatives**: keeping the always-visible toggle chip (a green ✓ chip for a finished state is furniture — attention UI should vanish when there is nothing to attend to); auto-confirming unmeasurable rows (a PDF the machine cannot see is exactly what a human must look at).

## 2026-08-11 — multi-signal bold gate ships (owner: blanket human verification is not robust; gate on confidence)

Owner directed replacing the confirm-everything model with a measured confidence gate. Ran a bounded 3-round spike loop (scripts `samples/tools/bold-multisignal-*.mjs`, results `docs/bold-multisignal-r{1,2,3}.json`), objective: maximize auto-resolution subject to ZERO confident mistakes on held-out validation. Signals: raw stroke-width ratio (median min ink-run on a 3x upscaled, contrast-stretched warning crop), ink-density ratio, size-ratio sanity gate, AI stroke-weight judgment. Round 1 (OCR-dependent, capH-normalized): 21% coverage — normalization was cancelling the signal. Round 2 (exact DOM boxes for generated cases, crop-then-OCR for real labels, raw stroke ratio): **56% train / 36% validation coverage, 0 confident mistakes incl. 24 never-seen-font cases**; all-bold violation routes to human. Round 3 (stem filtering, always-upscale, geometric fallback): 58% train but 28% validation — overfit, rejected; loop stopped on the diminishing-returns rule. **Round 2's thresholds shipped**, frozen in `lib/compare/boldGate.ts` (unit-tested), measured client-side in `lib/boldMeasure.ts` (canvas + tesseract on the located warning band). Integration: confident-bold auto-resolves the glance (single check: Formatting row PASS "verified by measurement"; batch: "bold ✓ measured", excluded from the Confirm bold count; CSV `bold_check=auto_verified`); confident-not-bold escalates the row to Needs review; inconclusive keeps the human glance in the strip. Human decisions always override the machine. Live sample batch: 7/11 auto-verified, 4 routed to human, 0 false calls. Known definitional edge (documented): semibold-600 prefixes auto-resolve as bold. **Rejected alternatives**: round-3 config (better train score, worse validation — the definition of overfit); shipping a softmax-style confidence without the zero-mistake constraint (a confident mistake on a federal warning is the one unrecoverable error); training a bespoke CNN (no held-out realism guarantee inside the take-home window — named as the production path if more coverage is needed).

## 2026-08-11 — densitometry validation matrix KILLS the bold auto-verify idea (owner pushed for wider evidence)

Owner asked whether the 11-label densitometry result generalized (other fonts? resolutions? real submission formats?). Researched the governing reg first — 27 CFR 16.22 requires "GOVERNMENT WARNING" caps+bold, **the remainder may NOT be bold** (a check we don't have — flagged as follow-up), type minimums 1/2/3 mm by container size with 40/25/12 max characters per inch, contrasting background; TTB COLA guidance: JPEG/PNG only, ≤1.5 MB, ~120–170 dpi, JPEG quality ~"70". Built a 48-case matrix (`samples/tools/bold-densitometry-matrix.mjs`, results `docs/bold-densitometry-matrix.json`): 6 fonts × bold/regular, 3 type sizes, 3 resolutions, JPEG-q70, dark background, semibold-600 boundary, all-bold violation, and a COLA-typical combo (0.75× + JPEG q70 ≈ the recommended dpi band). **The method fails**: (1) no cross-font threshold exists — bold Times measures 1.45 while non-bold Georgia/Verdana measure 1.53 (font design contributes as much ink-density signal as weight); (2) at COLA-typical quality, 3 of 4 measurable bold prefixes read as NOT bold (1.26–1.38) — the same directional bias blur showed, triggered by TTB's own recommended submission format; (3) 10px type and 0.5× resolution are unmeasurable; (4) the all-bold violation case defeats the metric structurally (it is a ratio against a body reference assumed non-bold — circular when the body's boldness is itself in question); (5) it can't distinguish semibold from bold. The earlier 11/11 was an artifact of a single-font, sharp, uniform-resolution sample set. **Decision: no pixel-based bold auto-verify ships.** The AI stroke-weight judgment (16/17, robust to font/format/resolution in ways pixel heuristics are not) stays the advisory signal, human confirmation stays, and the right investment is making the human step cheap (batch bold spot-check strip, deferred roadmap). **Rejected alternatives**: shipping two-signal auto-verify on the 11-label evidence (would have auto-verified wrongly on Times-style serifs and auto-flagged clean bold labels submitted at TTB's own recommended quality); tuning per-font thresholds (requires font identification — heavier and more fragile than the AI judgment it would corroborate); validating against real COLA-registry label images (the public registry is a form-driven portal; noted as the next validation step if this is ever revisited).
