### Performance
- **P1 — MET** ~5s per label MEASURED on deployed URL (incl. evidence-band call): **p50 4.3s, worst 4.5s across n=6 successful runs** — note this is worst-of-successful: `scripts/measure.ts` excludes non-OK responses, and [approach.md](approach.md) §limitations 6 records a cold start at 9.3s, a warm worst at 9.6s, and one run that never returned inside 45s. The ~5s figure is a median, not a ceiling; warning-*failing* labels also answer in **~4.0s** (measured live) — their second confirming reading runs asynchronously via `/api/confirm` (measured 5.4s) and updates the warning row in place, so the false-rejection guard (U4) costs no waiting; batch rows keep the blocking pass (~8s/row, absorbed by the batch wall-clock) — raw timings in [measured-performance.json](measured-performance.json)
- **P2 — MET** Batch 200–300 first-class — batch review page: CSV+files dropzone, loud pairing, filters/search/pagination, detail panel, sample batch
- **P3 — MET** 250-label batch **135s wall-clock, 250/250 ok, 0 rate-limited, 0 errors** on deployed app — [measured-performance.json](measured-performance.json)

### UX
- **U1 — MET** Non-technical 50+ usable — 3-click demo verified in browser: sample → rendered verdict (single) and sample batch → triage, zero downloads/instructions; UX cold-read at final gate
- **U2 — MET** Loud human-readable errors — type/size guards, refusal/timeout/429 copy ([lib/vision/extract.ts](../lib/vision/extract.ts) `failureMessage`), not-a-label card, CSV-format and pairing errors
- **U3 — MET** Triage 250 without drowning — count-carrying filter chips (All / Matched / Need review / Not required; a row still owing a bold glance counts under Need review, so a green tick always means finished), one-click **Need review** filter + search, master-detail table (row click opens the evidence panel with an Audit trail tab and a **Your review** bar — *Reviewed — OK* / *Needs correction* — that outranks machine triage; Review next steps through), **Download report** (CSV with `agent_review` and `bold_check` columns), refresh guard; verified in browser on sample batch
- **U4 — MET** Dave's "don't make my life harder" = false-rejection aversion, designed in — tri-state `unreadable` ≠ `missing` (glare → "check manually", never a fail); second-reading confirmation on warning failures, plus four gates added after large-scale measurement — deviation-agreement (two readings must agree *which* word differs), legibility (no word-for-word claim on text that could not be read), disputed-absence, and statement-boundary trimming — measured **42 false rejections across 1,360 synthetic degradations (7.5% of compliant samples)**, concentrated in tear/occlusion where the warning is physically destroyed ([robustness-matrix.json](robustness-matrix.json)); on **156 scored real approved TTB labels** the tool's own false-rejection rate is **4 of 156 (2.6%)** ([real-labels.json](real-labels.json), causes in [real-labels-causes.json](real-labels-causes.json)): all 40 `fail_missing` verdicts were verified correct by direct inspection on contact sheets — front, neck or EU-market labels carrying no US warning — recorded per-label in [samples/real/manifest.json](../samples/real/manifest.json) (`has_warning: false` + `excluded_reason`). Those 40 are therefore excluded from the scored set, which is why the denominator is 156 and not 196, and of the 22 remaining flags **18 are labels whose printed text genuinely departs from 27 CFR 16.21** — square brackets for parentheses, a period for a comma, a dropped "the" — confirmed by a second independent reading and spot-checked by eye. Only 4 are the tool reading the wrong region of a crowded label; verdict vocabulary surfaces, never auto-rejects; case/punct differences read "Match — formatting differs" ([approach.md](approach.md) §perception/verdict, [degraded-fidelity.json](degraded-fidelity.json))

## Soft requirements (writeup lines mandatory)
- **N1 — MET on the stretch goal as written; extended well past it** — the brief asks to "tolerate imperfect photos (weird angles, bad lighting, glare)". Scored on exactly those three: **544 images, 516 safe (94.9%), ZERO false rejections** ([robustness-matrix.json](robustness-matrix.json)). Testing then went beyond the ask — occlusion, tearing, creasing, curvature, sensor noise, colour cast and heavy downscaling — and **every one of the 42 false rejections in the full matrix comes from those extra conditions**, none from the three the brief names. Full detail — **1,360 synthetic degradations** (34 scoreable labels × 40 conditions: blur, angle, rotation, low light, overexposure, JPEG, downscale, glare, shadow, crease, curvature, tear, occlusion, sensor noise, colour cast, combined): **1,257/1,360 safe (92.4%), 42 false rejections (7.5% of 560 compliant samples), 61 violations not caught (7.6% of 800 violation samples)** ([robustness-matrix.json](robustness-matrix.json)). False rejections concentrate in physical destruction — occlusion (20) and tear (18) — where part of the warning is genuinely gone. Of the 61 not caught, **56 are the bold advisory**; re-scored through the shipped stroke-width gate that number falls to **3 of 160 (1.9%)** ([bold-gate-rescore.json](bold-gate-rescore.json)) — the matrix reads the raw server advisory, a layer the UI never shows. Also measured against **200 harvested real TTB COLA labels, 156 of them scored** ([real-labels.json](real-labels.json)). The COLA corpus mixes flat print files with genuine photographs (curved labels on bottles, angled foil stamps with real shadow), so real optics are represented; what it structurally cannot contain is **degraded capture** — nobody submits a torn or obscured photo to a regulator, which is why the synthetic matrix carries that axis. Real photographs of *damaged* labels remain the honest gap — [approach.md](approach.md) §limitations item 4
